
# Lannkz YouTube Performance Analysis — Findings

## Research Question

What makes viewers click, what makes them stay,
and what should change in future uploads?

## Finding 1: Scouting Reports Show Strong Typical Performance

Evidence:
- 21 Scouting Report videos analyzed.
- Median engaged views: 30,175.
- Average retention: 44.24%.
- Average CTR: 4.96%.

Interpretation:
Scouting Reports have the highest median engaged views
among the five content categories and the highest average
percentage viewed.

Limitation:
These are observational results. Differences in video age,
topics, and audience demand may influence performance.

Potential recommendation:
Continue producing Scouting Reports as a core format
while testing improvements to titles, thumbnails,
and viewer retention.



## Finding 2: Longer Videos Generate More Watch Time per View

Evidence:
- Under 10 minutes (27 videos):
  - Average retention: 45.13%
  - Average view duration: 3.60 minutes

- 10–15 minutes (25 videos):
  - Average retention: 41.97%
  - Average view duration: 4.88 minutes

- 15+ minutes (6 videos):
  - Average retention: 32.57%
  - Average view duration: 6.40 minutes

Interpretation:
Longer videos have lower average percentage retention,
but viewers spend more minutes watching them.

Limitation:
Only six videos exceed 15 minutes, and these were
published during an earlier period of the channel.
Differences in topics, audience size, and publication
dates may influence the results.

Potential recommendation:
Avoid shortening videos solely to improve percentage
retention. Choose video length based on the amount
of analysis the topic requires, and evaluate both
percentage retention and average view duration.



## Finding 3: The CTR-Retention Relationship Varies by Content Type

Evidence:
- Across all 58 videos, CTR and average percentage
  viewed have a correlation of -0.337.

- Correlation by content type:
  - Scouting Report (21 videos): +0.309
  - General Analysis (14 videos): -0.627
  - Tactical Analysis (12 videos): -0.535
  - Match Analysis (9 videos): +0.116
  - Theory / Prediction (2 videos): -1.000

- After excluding the lowest-retention video,
  General Analysis still shows a correlation of -0.604.

Interpretation:
The relationship between click-through rate and
retention differs across content formats.

General Analysis and Tactical Analysis show negative
relationships, while Scouting Reports show a positive
relationship.

This suggests that packaging and viewing behaviour
may interact differently across formats.

Limitations:
Correlation does not establish causation.

CTR and retention may both be influenced by audience
composition, video length, topics, and distribution.

The Theory / Prediction category has only two videos,
so its correlation is not meaningful for generalization.

Potential recommendation:
Review high-CTR, low-retention General Analysis and
Tactical Analysis videos to investigate whether their
opening sections deliver on the expectations created
by their titles and thumbnails.

Avoid assuming that higher CTR causes lower retention.



## Finding 4: Release-Relevant Videos Show Higher Average Reach

Evidence:
- Low release relevance (40 videos):
  - Average CTR: 4.84%
  - Average retention: 42.96%
  - Average engaged views: 44,426

- Medium release relevance (6 videos):
  - Average CTR: 5.12%
  - Average retention: 42.35%
  - Average engaged views: 25,187

- High release relevance (12 videos):
  - Average CTR: 5.14%
  - Average retention: 40.88%
  - Average engaged views: 75,812

Interpretation:
Videos closely connected to recent Blue Lock
developments have slightly higher average CTR
and substantially higher mean engaged views
than low-relevance videos.

However, they also have slightly lower
average percentage retention.

Limitations:
A few highly successful videos inflate the
average engaged views in the High category.

Differences in video age, topic, content type,
and audience demand may influence the results.

These findings do not establish that publishing
timely content causes higher viewership.

Potential recommendation:
Maintain a mix of timely and evergreen uploads.

Use major manga developments as opportunities
for relevant analysis, while continuing to produce
evergreen formats such as Scouting Reports.
